Preparing WebGPU…

Each run starts with a fresh scene and keeps its solving settings. The checks make sure every contact fits and object positions stay valid. Smaller times are better. These times measure physics, not drawing or overall FPS. Cloth keeps its fabric model in both modes. “Checks passed” covers the correctness checks only. Lower milliseconds are better. “Slower steps” is the time that 95 out of 100 steps fit within. “CPU + wait” includes submission, GPU work and copying measurements back; it is a separate elapsed-time measurement. Peak contacts counts touching points across warmup and measured steps. Compare collision time alongside contact count and scene settings; body count alone does not describe collision load. The scene column shows how much simulated time each physics step advances. A smaller step needs more updates to cover one second. For example, the 240 Hz ragdoll net needs four steps per 60 Hz frame; multiply its per-step time by four before adding drawing time.

Babylon or direct WebGPU drawing?

Both renderers draw the same GPU world at 960 × 540 pixels. Babylon uses thin instances and GPU copies, with no body pose downloads or CPU matrix updates. The comparison alternates drawing order and keeps the camera, geometry, shaders and physics identical. Antialiasing and debug overlays are off.
